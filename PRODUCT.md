# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Engineers, hiring managers, and collaborators who arrive from open-source projects (Dozzle, dtop), GitHub, LinkedIn, or word of mouth. They land here to evaluate one of two things: what side projects Amir is building, or who Amir is as an engineer-leader. They are technical, design-literate, and quick to bounce if a personal site feels generic.

## Product Purpose

A personal site for Amir Raminfar, engineering manager at Netflix and prolific open-source builder (Dozzle, dtop, phantomjs-node, Clashleaders, Gruper). It exists to make the work and the person feel coherent: side projects on one side, identity and trajectory on the other, both rendered in a way that itself proves taste and craft. Success is when a visitor leaves with a clear, warm impression of Amir as someone who ships, mentors, and builds with care, not a generic "engineering manager" resume.

## Brand Personality

Warm, innovative, modern. Voice is first-person and direct, not corporate. Tone leans confident but unpretentious, the kind of writing an engineer-leader uses when they have nothing to prove. The interface should feel like it was hand-built by the person it represents, with small, considered moments of cleverness (the wave animation, view transitions, raindrops) that reward attention without demanding it.

## Anti-references

- Generic blog templates with reverse-chronological post lists and no point of view.
- Standard dev-portfolio aesthetic: mono font, blinking terminal cursor, neon-on-black, "$ whoami" headers.
- The FAANG-manager LinkedIn look: stock photos, badge logos, "passionate leader" copy, no work shown.
- Glassy SaaS landing pages with hero metrics, identical card grids, gradient text.
- "Random website with no interesting data" — pages that exist to fill a route rather than show something worth seeing.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** The site itself is the proof of craft. Custom animations, view transitions, and considered typography do the work that adjectives would do on a weaker site. Never write copy that claims taste the design hasn't already demonstrated.
2. **Warm-modern, not cold-tech.** Cream and warm accents (brown, gold) over the dev-portfolio reflex of dark mode + neon. When dark mode is used, it stays warm-tinted, never blue-black. The category-reflex answer is wrong here on purpose.
3. **Engineer-craftsman, not corporate manager.** Lean into the open-source builder identity. Projects, code, and signature visual effects come first; titles and company logos are supporting cast.
4. **Substance over filler.** Every page earns its existence. Three real pages with real content beat ten skeleton routes. No "blog" section unless there is something genuinely worth reading.
5. **Quiet signature moments.** One or two distinctive flourishes per surface (the wave system, raindrops, view-transition title morph), not a parade of effects. Innovation is felt, not announced.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific requirements beyond reasonable defaults. Maintain readable contrast in both light and dark modes; do not regress existing keyboard navigation.
